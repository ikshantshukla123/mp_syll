Chapter 01 · Prehistory

Before the kings, there were artists

Long before Ujjain had a king or Khajuraho had a temple, someone stood in a rock shelter in the Vindhyas and painted a hunting scene. That is where MP's history begins — not with a dynasty, but with a human hand on stone.

S
Story hook

Bhimbetka is not one painting. It is a gallery with layers: a Paleolithic hunt, a Mesolithic gathering, a Chalcolithic horseman, a medieval soldier — sometimes on the same wall, one age painted over another. Standing there is like scrolling through 10,000 years of human life.

Chapter 02 · Ancient MP

Avanti, Ashoka and the first great cities

Ancient MP was not a backwater. Ujjain was one of India's great cities — a capital, a trading hub, and a crossroads of armies and ideas. When Ashoka was still a prince, he was sent here to govern.

The Mahajanapada years

Of the sixteen Mahajanapadas, two matter directly for MP: Avanti, with its capital at Ujjain, and Chedi, in the Bundelkhand region. Avanti was ruled by the Pradyota dynasty and later absorbed into the Magadhan empire. Ujjain's position on trade routes made it a prize for every northern power.

Ashoka and Sanchi

According to Buddhist tradition, Ashoka was sent to Ujjain as a governor before he became emperor. His wife Devi came from Vidisha, and his children Mahendra and Sanghamitra carried Buddhism to Sri Lanka. Sanchi, near Vidisha, became the great Buddhist site of central India: Ashoka raised the original Great Stupa, and later generations added the gateways and railings.

Ashokan edicts in MP are found at Sanchi, Rupnath (Jabalpur), Panguraria (Sehore) and Gujarra (Datia). Sanchi is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1989).

E
Exam lens

Classic one-liners: Ashoka was governor of Ujjain; Sanchi's Great Stupa was begun by Ashoka; Heliodorus pillar is at Besnagar (Vidisha); Bharhut is in Satna district.

After the Mauryas

Shunga

Vidisha and Bharhut

After the Mauryas, the Shungas patronised Buddhist art. The Bharhut stupa railings (Satna) are among the finest early Indian sculptures; many pieces are now in the Indian Museum, Kolkata.

Indo-Greek

The Heliodorus pillar

At Besnagar (Vidisha), the Greek ambassador Heliodorus erected a Garuda pillar in honour of Vasudeva around 113 BCE. It shows how far trade and diplomacy reached.

Shaka

Ujjain and the Satraps

The Western Satraps ruled Malwa with Ujjain as a key centre. Rudradaman is the most famous Shaka ruler; the Shaka era (78 CE) is used in their inscriptions.

Kushana

Coins and connections

Kushana coins and art travelled through central India. Their empire linked MP to the silk routes and to Mathura's artistic world.

!
Trap alert

Sanchi's stupa was begun by Ashoka, but its famous toranas (gateways) were added later, in the Satavahana period. Also, the Heliodorus pillar is in Vidisha, not Ujjain.

M
Memory hook

A-U-C: Avanti → Ujjain → Chandragupta's empire. And Ashoka's first big posting was Ujjain.

Chapter 03 · Guptas to Harsha

The golden age, and the storm after it

The Gupta age is called India's golden age. In MP, it left some of its finest stone: the Udayagiri caves, the Bagh paintings, and the temples of Eran. Then came the Hunas — and a local king named Yashodharman.

The Gupta footprint

Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) made Ujjain a second capital. The Udayagiri caves near Vidisha, with their monumental Varaha panel, belong to his reign; an inscription there records his minister Virasena. Kalidasa, the greatest Sanskrit poet, is traditionally associated with Ujjain and the Gupta court. The Bagh caves in Dhar district preserve Gupta-period Buddhist paintings.

Kumaragupta I is linked to the Mandasor inscription (436–437 CE), which records the construction of a sun temple by a guild of silk weavers. It is a rare window into the lives of merchants and craftsmen, not just kings.

The Huna storm

In the 5th–6th centuries, the Hunas pushed into India. Toramana is known from an inscription on the Varaha image at Eran; his son Mihirakula became the stuff of legend. The Aulikara king Yashodharman of Dashapura (Mandsaur) defeated Mihirakula around 528 CE. His victory pillar at Sondani (Mandsaur) is one of the great political inscriptions of ancient India.

E
Exam lens

Three names to keep straight: Yashodharman defeated Mihirakula; Bhanugupta's Eran inscription (510 CE) is the earliest epigraphic reference to Sati; the Aulikaras (called "Olinkar" in the syllabus) ruled from Dashapura/Mandsaur.

Chapter 04 · The Rajput age

Temples, tigers and rival kings

Between the 9th and 13th centuries, MP was a patchwork of Rajput kingdoms. They fought each other, built temples that still define Indian architecture, and gave the world Raja Bhoja and the Khajuraho temples.

Malwa · Dhara

Paramara

  • Founder: Upendra; capital at Dhara (Dhar).
  • Raja Bhoja (c. 1010–1055): the greatest ruler; built the Bhojshala at Dhar and the Bhojpur temple; credited with Bhojtal (Upper Lake, Bhopal).
  • Author of Samarangana Sutradhara, Shringara Manjari and medical works.
  • Last ruler Mahalakadeva was defeated by Alauddin Khalji's forces in 1305.
Bundelkhand · Khajuraho

Chandela

  • Founder: Nannuka; later capital at Mahoba.
  • Yashovarman, Dhanga and Vidyadhara expanded the kingdom; Vidyadhara resisted Mahmud Ghazni at Kalinjar.
  • Khajuraho temples built c. 950–1050 CE; UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986).
  • Parmal (Paramardideva) was defeated by Prithviraj Chauhan in 1182; the kingdom ended under Qutb-ud-din Aibak.
Mahakoshal · Tripuri

Kalachuri

  • Capital: Tripuri (Tewar, near Jabalpur).
  • Kokalla I, Gangeyadeva and Karna were major rulers.
  • Patrons of temples; the Chausath Yogini temple at Bhedaghat is linked to the Kalachuri period.
  • Their inscriptions use the Chedi era.
Gwalior

Tomar

  • Man Singh Tomar (1486–1516) was the greatest ruler.
  • Built the Man Mandir palace and the Gujari Mahal for his queen Mrignayani.
  • Fought Sikandar Lodi; Tomar rule effectively ended when Ibrahim Lodi captured Gwalior in 1516.
Gwalior–Morena

Kachhapaghata

  • Feudatories of the Gurjara-Pratiharas; core region Gopachal (Gwalior–Morena–Shivpuri–Guna).
  • Gwalior branch: Vajradaman, Kirtiraj, Mahipal, Devapala.
  • Kirtiraj built the Kakanmath temple at Sihoniya; Mahipal built the Sas-Bahu temple (1093); Devapala is linked to the Chausath Yogini temple at Mitaoli.
  • Other branches: Narwar (Sumitra) and Dubkund (Yuvaraja).
Kannauj · overlords

Gurjara-Pratihara

  • Nagabhata I founded the dynasty's power; Mihira Bhoja (836–885) was its greatest ruler.
  • Their empire centred on Kannauj; the Kachhapaghatas began as their feudatories.
  • Do not confuse Mihira Bhoja with Raja Bhoja of the Paramaras.
E
Exam lens

The most repeated match in this section: Khajuraho → Chandela, Bhojshala/Bhojpur → Paramara, Tripuri → Kalachuri, Man Mandir → Tomar, Kakanmath/Sas-Bahu → Kachhapaghata.

!
Trap alert

Raja Bhoja (Paramara, Malwa) is not Mihira Bhoja (Gurjara-Pratihara, Kannauj). Khajuraho is Chandela, not Bundela. The Bundelas come later, at Orchha.

M
Memory hook

P-C-K-T-K: Paramara–Dhar, Chandela–Khajuraho, Kalachuri–Tripuri, Tomar–Gwalior, Kachhapaghata–Morena.

Art and architecture spotlight

Khajuraho

Built by the Chandelas between c. 950 and 1050 CE. The temples are in the Nagara style, with curvilinear shikharas and richly carved exteriors. The largest is the Kandariya Mahadeva; other famous temples include Lakshmana, Vishwanath, Chitragupta, Chaturbhuj, Parsvanath and Adinath.

Gwalior

The fort is often called the "Gibraltar of India". Its layered architecture includes the Teli ka Mandir (Pratihara period), the Sas-Bahu temples (Kachhapaghata), the Man Mandir palace and Gujari Mahal (Tomar), and the Gopachal rock-cut Jain statues.

Chapter 05 · Medieval MP

Mandu, the Gonds and the Bundelas

Medieval MP has three great stories: a Sultanate that built a city of joy on a plateau, a Gond queen who chose death over surrender, and a Bundela chief who made a Mughal emperor's friendship pay.

Mandu and the Malwa Sultanate

The Malwa Sultanate was founded by Dilawar Khan Ghuri in 1401, with its capital at Mandu (Dhar district). Under Hoshang Shah and the Khalji sultans, Mandu became a city of palaces, pavilions and lakes. Ghiyath Shah built the famous Jahaz Mahal, a palace shaped like a ship between two lakes.

The last great ruler, Baz Bahadur, is remembered with the singer-poet Rani Roopmati. In 1562, Akbar's general Adham Khan defeated Baz Bahadur and annexed Malwa.

The Gonds and Rani Durgavati

The Gonds ruled four major kingdoms: Garha-Mandla (Jabalpur), Deogarh (Chhindwara–Nagpur region), Chanda (Chandrapur) and Kherla (Betul). Sangram Shah expanded Garha-Mandla, and his son Dalpat Shah married Rani Durgavati, a Chandela princess.

After Dalpat Shah's death in 1550, Durgavati ruled as regent for her young son Vir Narayan. In 1564, the Mughal general Asaf Khan invaded. Durgavati fought at Narrai, near Jabalpur, and when defeat became certain, she took her own life on 24 June 1564. Her son died fighting; the kingdom passed to Mughal control.

S
Story hook

Durgavati's motto, as remembered in the chronicles, was that it was better to die with honour than live with disgrace. She rode into battle on her elephant, Sarman. When she was wounded, she refused to be carried away — and chose her dagger over capture.

The Bundelas and Orchha

Orchha was founded in 1531 by Rudra Pratap Singh. Its golden age came under Vir Singh Deo (1605–1627), who built the Jahangir Mahal and the Raj Mahal. Later, Jujhar Singh rebelled against Shah Jahan and was crushed. The most famous Bundela ruler was Chhatrasal (1649–1731) of Panna, who fought Aurangzeb and later allied with the Maratha Peshwa Baji Rao I.

The Baghelas of Rewa

The Baghela dynasty, founded by Vyaghra Deo, ruled Rewa and the Baghelkhand region. Raja Ramchandra Singh was a great patron; Tansen, the legendary musician, was associated with his court before joining Akbar. In the 20th century, Rewa's Maharaja Martand Singh is remembered for the discovery of the white tiger (Mohan) in 1951.

The Mughal conquest

Akbar's forces took Malwa (1562), Gondwana (1564) and Asirgarh (1601). Burhanpur became an important Mughal base in the Deccan; Mumtaz Mahal died there in 1631. Aurangzeb's long Deccan campaigns also passed through MP.

E
Exam lens

Durgavati = 1564, Narrai, Asaf Khan. Mandu = Dhar. Orchha = 1531, Rudra Pratap Singh. Chhatrasal = Panna, Baji Rao I.

!
Trap alert

Durgavati was a Chandela princess married into the Gond royal family — not a Gond by birth. She fought Asaf Khan, not Akbar in person. And Mandu is in Dhar, not Indore.

M
Memory hook

1562 MALWA, 1564 GONDWANA, 1601 ASIRGARH. Two years, one queen, one fort.

Chapter 06 · Marathas & princely states

Three houses, three styles of power

The 18th century belonged to the Marathas. In MP, three very different states emerged: the Scindias of Gwalior, the Holkars of Indore, and the Begums of Bhopal.

Gwalior

Scindia

  • Founder: Ranoji Scindia; the family served the Peshwas.
  • Mahadaji Scindia rebuilt the family's power after 1761.
  • Daulat Rao Scindia lost the Battle of Assaye (1803) to Arthur Wellesley; the Treaty of Surji-Anjangaon followed.
  • Later rulers: Jayajirao, Jiwajirao, Madhavrao.
Indore

Holkar

  • Founder: Malhar Rao Holkar.
  • Ahilyabai Holkar (1725–1795; ruled 1767–1795) made Maheshwar her capital and patronised temples, ghats and weaving.
  • Yashwant Rao Holkar fought the British; the Battle of Mahidpur (1817) and the Treaty of Mandsaur (1818) ended Holkar independence.
Bhopal

The Begums

  • Founder: Dost Mohammad Khan.
  • Bhopal was ruled by a remarkable line of women: Qudsia Begum, Sikandar Begum, Shah Jahan Begum and Sultan Jahan Begum.
  • The state supported the British in 1857; it merged with India in 1949 and into MP in 1956.
  • Landmark: Taj-ul-Masajid, one of India's largest mosques.

Other important states included Dewas (Pawar), Nagpur (Bhonsle; now in Maharashtra), Orchha, Panna, Rewa, Datia, Samthar and Charkhari. After the Anglo-Maratha wars, most of these became princely states under British paramountcy.

The British framework

The Central Provinces were created in 1861, with Nagpur as their capital. The Central India Agency coordinated the British relationship with the princely states of Malwa, Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand. This split — directly ruled districts plus princely states — shaped MP's freedom movement: in British districts, people fought the Raj; in princely states, they also fought their own rulers through praja mandals.

Exam lens

The match to memorise: Scindia → Gwalior, Holkar → Indore, Bhopal → Begums. Ahilyabai's capital was Maheshwar; the Battle of Mahidpur (1817) and the Treaty of Mandsaur (1818) ended Holkar power.

!

Ahilyabai's capital was Maheshwar, not Indore. Bhopal was a princely state, not part of British India proper. And the Battle of Mahidpur belongs to the Third Anglo-Maratha War.

Ahilyabai: the builder-queen

Ahilyabai Holkar is one of MP's most inspiring rulers. After the death of her husband and son, she took charge of the state and ruled for nearly three decades. She rebuilt temples and ghats across India — including Kashi Vishwanath — and made Maheshwar a centre of learning, craft and the famous Maheshwari saree.

Chapter 07 · 1857 & tribal resistance

The fire in the forests

In 1857, the revolt did not stay in the plains. It ran through the forests of Nimar and Gondwana, and it was led by people whose names are still sung in MP's villages.

1857 in MP

The revolt touched Neemuch, Sagar, Jabalpur, Gwalior, Indore and Bhopal. Rani Avantibai of Ramgarh (Mandla–Dindori region) led a fierce resistance after the British annexed her state; she died in 1858. In Jabalpur, the Gond rulers Shankar Shah and his son Raghunath Shah were executed by cannon on 18 September 1857. Tantya Tope was captured near Paron (Shivpuri) and hanged at Shivpuri on 18 April 1859. Rani Lakshmibai died at Gwalior on 18 June 1858.

S
Story hook

Shankar Shah and Raghunath Shah were tied to the mouths of cannons. According to eyewitness accounts, their faces showed no fear. The British meant the execution to terrorise Jabalpur; instead, it became one of the most powerful symbols of resistance in Gondwana.

Rani Avantibai: the queen of Ramgarh

Rani Avantibai Lodhi ruled Ramgarh in the Mandla–Dindori region. When the British applied the Doctrine of Lapse after her husband's death, she refused to surrender and raised an army. She fought the British in 1857–58 and chose death over capture. Her memorial at Ramgarh is a reminder that the revolt had powerful women leaders in the forests as well as in the plains.

Chapter 08 · Freedom movement

A flag, a forest, and a split in the Congress

MP's freedom story is not a footnote to the national story. A flag satyagraha in Jabalpur became a nationwide agitation, a forest satyagraha replaced salt in the landlocked centre, and the Congress's biggest internal crisis played out at Tripuri.

1920

Nagpur session of the Congress ratifies the Non-Cooperation programme; Mahatma Gandhi's leadership is confirmed. Nagpur was then part of MP.

1923

Jabalpur Flag Satyagraha (18 March). Pandit Sundarlal leads the demand to hoist the national flag; Subhadra Kumari Chauhan is remembered as the first woman arrested. The movement spreads to Nagpur and becomes a national Flag Satyagraha.

1930

Jungle Satyagraha. In a landlocked region with no salt to make, people break forest laws instead. Betul (Ghoda Dongri, under Ganjan Singh Korku), Chhindwara (Ramakona, under Badal Bhoi) and Seoni become centres.

1931

Charan Paduka firing (14 January), Chhatarpur. Troops fire on an unarmed gathering; the incident is remembered as the "Jallianwala Bagh of MP".

1939

Tripuri session (near Jabalpur). Subhash Chandra Bose defeats Pattabhi Sitaramayya (1580–1377) for the Congress presidency. The Pant resolution forces a crisis; Bose resigns, and the Forward Bloc is formed in May 1939.

1942

Quit India Movement spreads across MP; students, workers and tribal communities join.

1949

Bhopal merger movement succeeds; the princely state of Bhopal merges with India.

People to know

Revolutionary

Chandrashekhar Azad

Born in Bhabhra, Alirajpur (1906). A leading revolutionary; killed in Alfred Park, Allahabad, in 1931. MP's most famous link to the armed struggle.

Writer–freedom fighter

Makhanlal Chaturvedi

Born at Bhanpura (Narmadapuram). Editor of Karmavir; poet of Pushp ki Abhilasha; used literature as a weapon of nationalism.

Poet–satyagrahi

Subhadra Kumari Chauhan

Associated with Jabalpur; author of Jhansi ki Rani; among the first women arrested in the Flag Satyagraha.

Organiser

Pandit Sundarlal

Led the Jabalpur Flag Satyagraha; imprisoned for six months, and celebrated as "Tapasvi" Sundarlal.

Other important figures include Ravishankar Shukla (first Chief Minister), Dwarka Prasad Mishra, Seth Govind Das of Jabalpur, and the many tribal satyagrahis of Betul, Chhindwara and Seoni.

Chapter 09 · Formation of MP

The map is redrawn

Modern MP is a jigsaw puzzle assembled twice: once in 1956, when four pieces came together, and again in 2000, when Chhattisgarh walked away with the south-eastern piece.

Before 1956: four units

1956: the new state

The States Reorganisation Commission was appointed in 1953 under Fazl Ali, with K. M. Panikkar and H. N. Kunzru as members. Its report (1955) led to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. On 1 November 1956, the new Madhya Pradesh came into being by merging old MP (without Vidarbha), Madhya Bharat, Vindhya Pradesh and Bhopal. Bhopal became the capital; Nagpur went to Bombay State. Pandit Ravishankar Shukla became the first Chief Minister of the reorganised state, and Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya its first Governor.

Exam lens

Four facts that are asked again and again: 1 November 1956, Bhopal as capital, Ravishankar Shukla as first CM, and Pattabhi Sitaramayya as first Governor. Also remember that Nagpur was the old capital.

2000: Chhattisgarh

On 1 November 2000, the Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2000 created Chhattisgarh from 16 districts of the Raipur, Bilaspur and Bastar divisions. Raipur became its capital, and Ajit Jogi its first Chief Minister. MP became smaller but kept its place at the centre of India.

!
Trap alert

MP's capital was Nagpur until 1956, not Bhopal. Bhopal was initially part of Sehore district and became a separate district later. Do not confuse the 1956 reorganisation with the 2000 split.

M
Memory hook

1 NOV 1956 = MP. 1 NOV 2000 = CG. Same date, two different maps.

Why the date matters

MP celebrates Foundation Day on 1 November. The state's High Court is at Jabalpur, its capital is Bhopal, and its administrative structure today is divided into divisions and districts. For Mains, the key argument is that MP is a state built by integration: British provinces, princely states and a Part C state were stitched together on linguistic and administrative lines.

Buddhist

The Great Stupa, its four carved gateways (toranas), the Ashokan pillar and several monasteries. A complete timeline of Buddhist art from the Mauryan to the Gupta period.

Chandela

Bundela

Gupta

Early Buddhist

Bharhut

Satna district; Shunga-period stupa railings with some of the earliest narrative sculptures in Indian art.

Painting

MP's painting tradition runs from Bhimbetka's prehistoric rock art to the Bagh cave murals, and then to two living traditions: Gond painting, made famous by Jangarh Singh Shyam of Patangarh (Dindori), and Pithora painting, the ritual art of the Bhils, mastered by Pema Falya of Jhabua.

Music and dance

Music

  • Maihar gharana — Baba Allauddin Khan and his family made Maihar a global name in Hindustani music.
  • Kumar Gandharva — settled in Dewas; famous for Nirguni bhajans and a unique vocal style.

  • Matki — Malwa; dance with a pot.
  • Maanch — Malwa folk theatre.
  • Karma — Gond and Bhil ritual dance.
  • Bhagoria — Bhil festival and dance of Jhabua.
  • Saila and Baredi — Bundelkhand folk forms.

Part 3 · Medieval MP: the relay race

Medieval MP is easier when you see it as a relay race. The Delhi Sultanate carries the baton first, regional kingdoms take over, the Mughals arrive, and the Marathas pick up the pieces. Each handover leaves a fort, a title or a battle date behind.

1195–96

Muhammad Ghori enters MP. He defeats Lohanga Dev and captures Gwalior. By 1206, Gwalior, Kalinjar and Malwa are under Ghori's control.

1206

Qutb-ud-din Aibak founds the Slave dynasty and defeats Parimal Dev to capture Kalinjar.

1228

Iltutmish conquers Mandu, Gwalior, Malwa and the Mahakaleshwar temple at Ujjain.

1305

Ainul Mulk, a general of Alauddin Khalji, defeats Malhak Dev and ends Paramara power in Malwa.

1320

Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq founds the Tughlaq dynasty. The Batiagarh inscription records his palace at Damoh.

1398

The Tomaras take Gwalior. Raja Suraj Sen is credited with building the Gwalior fort; Man Singh Tomar later adds the Saas-Bahu and Gurjari Mahal.

1531

Gujarat conquers the Malwa Sultanate. In the same year, Rudra Pratap Bundela founds Orchha (the standard exam date).

1561

Baz Bahadur is defeated at Sarangpur by Akbar's generals Adam Khan and Pir Muhammad Khan. Malwa is annexed soon after.

1675

Chhatrasal takes Panna and makes it his capital, turning Bundelkhand into a powerful regional state.

1707

Aurangzeb dies. Mughal control weakens, and the Marathas expand across MP between 1720 and 1760.

Book Chapter 2 · History

Medieval MP: sultans, Rajputs and the Mandu moon

The medieval story is not one empire replacing another. It is a series of rooms: Delhi's sultans enter, Malwa's sultans settle, Gond and Bundela chiefs hold the forests and forts, the Mughals take the plains, and the Marathas inherit the fragments.

☾
The human hook

Mandu is remembered less for its walls than for Baz Bahadur and Rupmati: a music-loving sultan, a poet-singer, and a plateau city that still carries their names. In 1562 Akbar's army ended that chapter, but the love story outlived the kingdom.

The Delhi Sultanate reaches MP

After Ghori's victory at Tarain in 1192, his commanders pushed into the Gwalior and Bundelkhand belt. Iltutmish captured Gwalior in the 13th century; Alauddin Khalji's generals overran Malwa; Muhammad bin Tughlaq's experiments weakened the centre; and Timur's invasion of 1398 accelerated the break-up. That is the doorway through which regional sultanates walk.

The Malwa Sultanate

Dilawar Khan founded the Malwa Sultanate at Dhar around 1401. His son Hoshang Shah moved the capital to Mandu, which became the showpiece: the Jama Masjid, the tomb of Hoshang Shah, and a plateau full of palaces, baolis and gates. The Khalji sultans followed, and the last great figure was Baz Bahadur (1555–62), whose capital was Mandu and whose court was famous for music.

In 1562, Akbar's army defeated Baz Bahadur. Rupmati's story ends in tragedy. Mandu then became a Mughal outpost, and the centre of gravity shifted to the plains.

The geological layers

MP was part of Gondwanaland. Its rocks record a long sequence: Archaean granite in Bundelkhand; Dharwar metamorphics in Jabalpur, Balaghat and Chhindwara, rich in iron, manganese and other metals; Cuddapah and Vindhyan sandstones and limestones; Gondwana coal-bearing beds in the Satpura and Son-Mahanadi valleys; Bagh beds in the Narmada valley; and the Deccan Trap basalt that gives Malwa its black soil.

The 1956 settlement

The States Reorganisation Commission was set up in December 1953 under Fazl Ali, with H.N. Kunzru and K.M. Panikkar. Its report produced 14 states and 6 Union Territories. For MP, the key transfers were:

  • Eight Marathi-speaking districts — Nagpur, Wardha, Bhandara, Chanda, Akola, Amravati, Yavatmal and Buldhana — went to Bombay (now Maharashtra).
  • Sunel-Tappa went to Rajasthan.
  • Sironj tehsil of Kota district came to Vidisha district.
  • Madhya Bharat, Vindhya Pradesh and Bhopal merged into the new state.

Police and prisons

Book Chapter 20 · Judiciary

The High Court and the courts below it

India has one integrated judiciary. The MP High Court sits at the top of the state system, with permanent benches at Indore and Gwalior and a third at Bhopal.

Subordinate courts

  • District and Sessions Court — the same officer is District Judge for civil cases and Sessions Judge for criminal cases. The District Judge is appointed by the Governor in consultation with the High Court.
  • Special Courts — for SC/ST Act and anti-corruption cases; special courts under the 2011 Act at Bhopal, Indore, Gwalior and Jabalpur.

  • Labour/Industrial Court — Indore.

The MP State Human Rights Commission was established in September 1995 under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. Its chairperson is a retired Supreme Court judge or a retired High Court Chief Justice; its office is Paryawas Bhawan, Bhopal.

Book Chapter 21 · Panchayati Raj and urban bodies

Government at the village and city level

MP runs a three-tier Panchayati Raj system and a three-tier urban system. The 73rd Amendment covers panchayats; the 74th covers municipalities and corporations.

The MP Panchayati Raj Act, 1962 introduced the three tiers. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 gave panchayats constitutional status. MP’s Panchayati Raj Adhiniyam, 1993 was passed on 25 January 1994 and enforced on 20 August 1994. In 2007, MP became an early state to provide 50% reservation for women; women won about 52% of seats in 2009–10.

Gram Sabha meetings

Four sittings are mandatory: 15 August, 26 January, 30 January and 2 October. From 2013, an additional sitting is held on Ambedkar Jayanti. Panchayats have a five-year term; if dissolved early, elections must be held within six months.

Book Chapter 29 · Sports

Malkhamb, hockey and the medal winners

MP’s state sport is Malkhamb. Bhopal is the old nursery of hockey, Indore is the cricket and chess centre, and Gwalior has the women’s hockey academy.

!
State sport

Malkhamb was declared the state game on 9 April 2013. The pole is traditionally sheesham, polished with castor oil. It is performed on a pole, cane or rope.

Traditional games and centres

Traditional games include Gulli-danda, Kho-Kho, Sitoliya and Pitthu. Cricket’s Parsi Club was established at Indore in 1890; the Holkar Cricket Association in 1941 and MPCA in 1957. Bhopal is called the “nursery of hockey”. Snooker is said to have originated in Jabalpur, at the Narmada Club.
